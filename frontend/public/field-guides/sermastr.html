01

The one-paragraph version

SerMaStr is the agency's AI Director of SEO. It's a chat-based teammate — reachable in Slack and on its own dashboard page — that has read access to everything the suite knows about a client (rankings, Maps presence, AI-answer visibility, content produced, competitors, budget, backlinks, alerts, the SOPs) and can answer strategy questions the way a senior strategist would: opinionated, specific, citing the numbers and the playbook.

It can also take action — running scans, editing campaign settings, creating tasks, even commissioning a new page — but anything that costs money or changes something real is confirmed with you first. It doesn't schedule your day and it doesn't chase your team for status updates; that's PACE, its sibling agent (§8). SerMaStr's job is judgment: what should we do, and why.

03

What SerMaStr actually is

Under the hood, SerMaStr is given a system prompt that starts:

"You are SerMastr, the agency's Director of SEO — a senior strategist teammates come to for judgment, not just data."

That framing matters, because it explains the tone you'll get and the behavior to expect:

  • It has opinions, and it commits to them. Ask "what should we do about Acme's roof-repair keyword?" and you'll get a recommendation with a reason — not a bulleted list of neutral options. If your ask contradicts the data, it will push back and show you the number that changed its mind.
  • It decides instead of interrogating you. If you ask something underspecified, it makes the sensible professional call, states the assumption in one line so you can correct it, and moves on — it only stops to ask a real question when the answer would flip the whole recommendation and it genuinely can't infer it.
  • It's not always running. A full strategist review (the deepest, most expensive mode — §11) runs weekly for clients with something worth reviewing, or on demand, or when something serious happens. Day-to-day chat answers are a lighter-weight read of live data, not a full review every time.
  • It remembers, per client. SerMaStr can save a short durable note about a client — a decision made in conversation, a commitment, a fact the suite doesn't track — and bring it back up later, including following up on commitments that look overdue.
04

How SerMaStr thinks — four ideas worth understanding first

4.1 Everything is scoped to one client — unless you don't name one

Mention a client's name (or their domain) and SerMaStr pulls that client's full picture before answering. Say no client name, and it switches to portfolio mode — an agency-wide view (open alerts, freezes, unread notifications, open goals, across every client) so you can ask "who needs attention this week?" without naming anyone.

Why this matters

Without it, every morning check-in would mean naming clients one by one and hoping you didn't skip the one that's actually on fire. Portfolio mode is how you triage the whole book in one question instead.

A real portfolio-mode reply

Youwho needs attention this week?

SerMaStrThree things stand out. Coastal Plumbing is frozen — Google flagged a manual action on their homepage 4 days ago, nothing else should move until that's lifted. Acme Roofing has two open alerts stacking: a rank drop on "roof repair Springfield" and a Maps decline in the north-side grid — worth a real look today. BrightSmile Dental has nothing tracked at all — which usually means the setup got skipped, not that everything's fine. I'd start with the Coastal freeze. Want the detail on any of these?

Notice the order: freeze first, then the client stacking alerts, then the quiet client with nothing tracked — that's the triage priority SerMaStr always applies in portfolio mode.

4.2 It only knows what's in the suite — plus two escape hatches

SerMaStr's campaign numbers (rank, visibility, clicks, alerts) come only from the suite's own data or a live pull — never estimated, never guessed. Two things extend its reach without breaking that rule: a memory of things you've told it, and web search for public information the suite doesn't track (a competitor's reviews, industry news) — it always says where a fact came from, and never uses web search for the client's own campaign metrics.

Why this matters

A generic-sounding answer to "should we expand into City X" is worthless if it's actually a guess. SerMaStr's confidence tracks its evidence — when it has nothing, it says so instead of papering over the gap with plausible-sounding SEO advice.

The four-step "no data" pattern

Youshould we push into Shelbyville for Acme?

SerMaStrNothing for Shelbyville specifically — it's not a tracked keyword, hasn't had a geo-grid scan, and isn't in Acme's target cities. What I do have: Acme ranks #2 in Springfield for "roof repair," with strong pin coverage, so the same play has worked nearby. Which service should Shelbyville lead with? If it's roof repair, I can add it to the tracker and run a geo-grid scan centered there to get a real read before we commit to a page. Want me to do that?

Name the gap specifically → answer from the nearest real data → ask the one question that would let it be specific → offer, never run, the step that would close the gap.

4.3 It cites your playbook, not general SEO folklore

For any strategy-shaped question, SerMaStr's advice is required to come from the agency's written SOP library, cited inline (e.g. "How_To_Rank_In_Google_Maps SOP §Relevance"). If the SOPs are silent on something, it says so explicitly rather than making something up. Pure data lookups ("what's our rank for X") don't need a citation — that rule is for advice.

4.4 It reads the instruments correctly, every time

  • Rank position: lower is better. A missing GSC position for a day means no data that day — never "it dropped out."
  • Maps average rank is only over pins where the business was found. 3 found pins out of 25 at an average rank of 2.0 is barely present, not "ranking #2."
  • The GBP description is not a local-pack ranking factor — it matters for AI visibility instead.
  • A single AI-visibility engine flipping on one keyword is noise, not a trend. It reads batch rollups and cross-batch trends.

06

What SerMaStr can do — actions

SerMaStr doesn't just report — it can trigger real work. Every action falls into one of two buckets.

6.1 Free — runs immediately, no confirmation

  • Rebuild the Action Plan — no API spend, no side effects to guard.
  • Live Search Console pull — current/latest performance.
  • Cost-plan check — prices a proposed task set against the client's discretionary budget; always called before proposing anything that costs money.
  • Web search — public info only, bounded to a few per question.
  • SOP lookup — pulls a section not already loaded.
  • Maps/keyword history lookups, LeadOff city lookups — free reference reads.

6.2 Confirm-gated — SerMaStr stages it, you approve it

Anything that spends API budget or changes something real gets staged, not run:

"This will run a Maps geo-grid scan for Acme Roofing (2 paid DataForSEO calls). Reply yes to proceed."

Reply yes and it runs. Reply anything else, and the pending action is dropped. Note: the confirmation is per-conversation-thread — a new question before you reply "yes" cancels the pending action.

6.3 Worked examples

Commissioning content

Youcan we add "emergency roof tarping" as a tracked keyword for Acme, and also start a blog post on it

SerMaStrThat's not yet a tracked keyword or a page for Acme — I checked the tracker and the site inventory, nothing on that term. I'll add it to rank tracking and start a blog post targeting it. This will add 1 tracked keyword and start a content run for Acme Roofing. Reply yes to proceed.

Youyes

SerMaStrDone — "emergency roof tarping" is now tracked, and the blog run is queued. I'll ping you back once it's ready, or check Runs in the dashboard.

Diagnosing a drop — the "why," not just the alert

Youwhy did "roof repair Springfield" drop for Acme?

SerMaStrIt fell from #3 to #7 over the last 9 days. Two things line up: impressions on that query dropped too — not just position — and the latest SERP snapshot shows a competitor, Springfield Roof Pros, picked up a review spike and moved into the top 3. That traces to competitor movement (Rank_Drop_Mitigation_SOP_Organic §B5 step 6), not a technical issue on our side. I'd fund another link round on that page this month. Want me to save that to the Action Plan?

"What should we improve?" — a strategist-style answer, not a data dump

Youwhat should we improve for Acme this month?

SerMaStrThree things, ranked by leverage:

  1. Reoptimize "roof repair Springfield" — striking-distance at position 6, already found on 22 of 25 geo-grid pins, just not converting to page one. Cheapest win on the board.
  2. A location page for the north-side grid — average rank 9.4 across found pins, well behind the rest of the city. A coverage gap, not a ranking problem.
  3. Two AI-visibility keywords are invisible across every engine. Worth a GBP profile pass — AI Overviews lean heavily on GBP completeness.

SerMaStrThis month's task plan also has one unstaffed content line — I'd put a body on that before adding anything new. Want a full strategist review, or should I save these three as Action Plan steps?

Notice what each of these does that a vague answer wouldn't: it names the specific page or keyword, gives the number behind the claim, cites the SOP it's following, and ends with a concrete next move.

07

What SerMaStr can NOT do

Read this as carefully as §6 — a new hire's most common mistake is assuming SerMaStr is more autonomous, or less capable, than it actually is.

  • It never publishes to a client's live site. Commissioning content creates a draft deliverable — publishing is a separate, human step.
  • Some things are dashboard-only, on purpose: creating/archiving a client, freezing/lifting a freeze, WordPress/Drive credentials, brand-voice or ICP long-form text, reference page structures, the GBP OAuth connection.
  • A frozen client pauses content/link output — including SerMaStr's commission actions. It surfaces that prominently and won't route around it.
  • It doesn't override anyone else's precedence engine. The Director of Operations context is read-only insight — it can cite a conflict and offer to raise it, but has no authority over PACE, the reoptimization planner, or autonomy.
  • It doesn't run unattended. Every side-effecting or paid action is confirm-gated. A separate, much more conservative autonomy layer exists for a few narrow tasks on opted-in clients — a different system, off by default.
  • It won't re-judge a QA verdict, or re-derive a computed status. Goal status, QA pass/fail, and rank-drop classification are computed deterministically elsewhere; SerMaStr reports them, it doesn't second-guess them.
  • Anyone in the channel can trigger an action — it doesn't currently gate who can approve a confirm. Use judgment on a shared client.
  • It's not a search engine for the open web by default. It only reaches outside the suite when a question genuinely needs public information.

How SerMaStr fits with the other agents

The suite runs a small cast of AI teammates that share the same rails (Slack, the notifications service, the scheduler) but do different jobs.

QAJudges whether finished work is actually goodAutomatic on In QA, or on demandVerdicts only — never edits the deliverable
DORAFlags friction between the other three agentsDaily reconciliation + a weekly digestRead-only — opens a board task, never resolves it

Rule of thumb: ask SerMaStr "what should we do" questions and strategy. Ask PACE "is this task on track / who has capacity" questions. Never expect SerMaStr to chase a stalled task, and never expect PACE to explain why a ranking dropped.

09

Novice path — your first week

Do these in order. Each builds on the last.

  1. Say hello with a real client question — "How is Acme Roofing doing?" Read the reply structure: goal progress first, then 2–3 wins, then 2–3 concerns, each with a number.
  2. Ask a portfolio question with no client name — "Who needs attention this week?" Notice it triages the whole book on its own.
  3. Ask something it has no data for on purpose — a city or keyword never tracked. Read how it names the gap, answers from the nearest real data, asks one question, and offers (never runs) the step that would close it.
  4. Trigger your first free action — "Rebuild the Action Plan for Acme." No confirmation needed.
  5. Trigger your first confirm-gated action — ask it to add a tracked keyword. Read the confirmation closely, then reply yes.
  6. Read a strategist review, if one exists for a client you're on.

By the end of week one you should be comfortable asking questions and running the small stuff without checking this guide.

10

Intermediate path — building real proficiency

  1. Ask a "what should we improve" question and evaluate it against §4.3 — does it cite an SOP? Does it name the specific tool/page for each recommendation?
  2. Give it a fact it should remember, then start a new conversation later and ask about it. It should surface the memory unprompted.
  3. Ask a forecast question and a quick-win question, then compare the numbers against the Forecast page — they should match exactly.
  4. Have it commission a real deliverable and follow it through to the draft landing in the dashboard. Confirm you understand this is not the same as publishing.
  5. Practice the SOP-citation habit yourself — when it cites a section you haven't read, go read it.
  6. Watch what happens when you contradict the data — propose something the numbers argue against and see it push back with the number that changes its mind. This is designed behavior, not an error.
11

Advanced / proficient usage

  1. Run a full strategist review on purpose and read the whole thing — assessment, findings with SOP citations, proposals, open questions. Approve or dismiss a proposal and watch it become a real task via the PACE rails.
  2. Use portfolio mode as a standing morning habit — "what's on fire" as the first thing you ask each day.
  3. Learn to read a Director of Operations flag as a prompt to investigate, not an alarm — SerMaStr will never resolve it for you.
  4. Recognize the escalation triggers — a response episode open 6+ weeks with no improvement, or a client transitioning into sitewide decline, both auto-queue a strategist review.
  5. Understand budget-aware planning. If you ever see a plan proposed with dollar figures that don't add up against the client's discretionary budget, flag it.
  6. Know the boundary with autonomy. If you're unsure whether something happened because a teammate approved it or autonomy ran on its own, check the client's activity/notifications feed rather than assuming.

At this point you should be able to onboard the next new hire on SerMaStr yourself.

13

Practice exercises

Work through these with a real (or sandbox) client, novice → advanced. Don't skip ahead if an earlier one didn't go the way this guide describes.

Novice

  • Ask "how is <client> doing?" and identify the goal-progress lead, the wins, and the concerns.
  • Ask a portfolio question with no client named.
  • Ask about a city/keyword you know isn't tracked and confirm the four-step "no data" pattern.
  • Run the free "rebuild the Action Plan" action.
  • Run one confirm-gated action end to end (stage → read the confirm message → reply "yes").

Intermediate

  • Get it to cite a specific SOP section, then go actually read that section.
  • Teach it a memory, start a new conversation, and confirm it recalls the memory unprompted.
  • Ask for a 90-day forecast and a quick-win value, and check both against the Forecast page.
  • Commission a content run or Local SEO page and follow it from confirm → queued → drafted.
  • Propose something the data contradicts and read how it pushes back.

Advanced

  • Trigger and fully read a strategist review; approve one proposal and confirm it lands as a real task.
  • Find (or wait for) a Director of Operations flag and decide, correctly, whether it needs action or just awareness.
  • Explain to a teammate, without looking this guide up, the difference between SerMaStr, PACE, QA, and DORA.
  • Identify a case where SerMaStr correctly said "the SOPs are silent on this" instead of improvising.
14

Quick reference

Access

Slack channel (no @mention) · Slack DM · dashboard sidebar → SerMaStr → /assistant

Client scope

Name a client → single-client mode. Name none → portfolio (agency-wide) mode.

To trigger anything

Ask in plain English ("run a Maps scan for Acme," "add this keyword," "start a blog post about X"). Free actions run immediately; anything paid or side-effecting stages a confirmation — reply yes to proceed, anything else cancels it.

Never expects you to

Know an internal action name, write structured input, or pick a module by its technical key — describe what you want in plain language.

Always remember

It only reports what the suite actually knows, plus memory and public web search — never a guessed number. It advises and does small confirmed actions; it does not publish, does not override PACE/QA/the planner, and does not run unattended. For delivery/task-board questions, go to PACE instead.

Not the engineering spec (that's docs/modules/seo-strategist-agent-plan-v1_0.md) and not the task-board manual (that's the Task Manager guide, covering PACE) — this is the practical "how do I actually use this thing" reference.
