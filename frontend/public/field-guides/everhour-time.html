01

The one-paragraph version

Everhour is the stopwatch. The suite is the brain. You track your hours in Everhour — a lightweight time-tracking tool with a Chrome extension and a web app — against the clients and tasks you're working on. Once a day, the suite automatically pulls everyone's logged time and turns it into three things the agency actually uses: how much a client's work really costs, how full or empty each person's plate is, and how a task's actual time compared to what was estimated.

What Everhour is not. You don't manage your to-do list in Everhour — statuses, due dates, comments, and checklists all still live on the suite's Task board. Everhour has exactly one job: the clock.

Three things fall out of that diagram worth understanding up front:

  1. It's one-way. Everhour never tells you what to work on, and the suite never writes anything back into Everhour except the task names themselves (§04). All the thinking — status, due dates, priority — happens on the Task board.
  2. The project = the client, always. Doing work for Acme Roofing? You track it under the Acme Roofing project — never a personal or made-up project name.
  3. This isn't surveillance — it's a planning input. Nobody watches a live feed of your timer. The numbers exist so the agency can know what a client's work really costs, spot overload before it burns someone out, and make next month's estimates more accurate than a guess.
04

Where your tasks come from

Read this before you go looking for them — it saves you a "why can't I find my task" message to your PM.

Everhour normally plugs into tools like Asana or Trello, drawing a "start timer" button right next to each task on those sites. Our Task board is our own custom-built tool, so Everhour can't do that trick here. Instead, the suite does the opposite: the moment a task is created for you on the Task board, a lightweight copy of it — just the name and who it's assigned to — is automatically pushed into Everhour as a real task, sitting inside that client's project. That copy is what you click into and track time against.

  • Don't create your own tasks in Everhour. If you don't see the task you're looking for, it either hasn't synced yet, isn't assigned to you, or is a subtask/checklist item — only top-level tasks get a copy, so track time against the parent task.
  • Search by the exact task name from the Task board — the copy uses the same name.
  • Task predates this integration? Log time against the client's project with no specific task, and mention it to your PM.
  • The copy is metadata-only. Marking it "done" in Everhour does nothing to the real task — you still complete it on the Task board.

Example

Your PM creates a task called "Write blog post — roof maintenance checklist" on the Task board and assigns it to you, under the client Acme Roofing. Within a few minutes, a task with that exact name appears inside the Acme Roofing project in Everhour. That's the one you open and track time against — not a new task you type in yourself.

05

Day-one setup

Do this once, in order, before you track your first real hour.

5.1 — Get your account

Everhour accounts are provisioned by an admin, not self-serve. If you don't already have an invite email, ask your PM or admin to add you to the team. You'll set a password (or sign in with Google, if that's how the team is set up) the first time you log in at my.everhour.com.

5.2 — Get linked to your suite profile

Why this step matters most

The suite ties your Everhour identity to your dashboard login so your logged hours land on the right person's utilization and the right client's margin. An admin sets this on the Team page (Team & Capacity → your row → "Everhour user") — but it's worth confirming out loud in your first week: "Am I linked yet?" Unlinked, your time is still tracked correctly in Everhour itself — it just won't show up correctly inside the suite until someone links you.

5.3 — Install the Chrome extension

Official Chrome Web Store listing

Everhour — Time Tracking, Budgets, Expenses ↗

Confirm the publisher reads Everhour and the icon matches your invite email before installing — extension names get copied. Install trouble, or on Edge/Brave instead of Chrome? See Everhour's own Chrome install guide or Edge install guide.

  1. Open the listing above and click Add to Chrome → Add extension.
  2. Click the puzzle-piece icon in Chrome's toolbar and pin the Everhour icon so it's always visible — you'll use it constantly.
  3. Click the pinned icon and sign in with the account from §5.1.

You don't strictly need the extension — everything works from my.everhour.com too — but the extension is faster for the daily habit because it's always one click away.

5.4 — Take a lap around the web app

Log in once and find Projects (your clients — confirm you can see the ones you're assigned to), My Timesheet (your own day-by-day log), and Reports (mostly a PM/admin surface, but good to know it exists).

06

The daily habit — step by step

The part you'll do dozens of times a week. There are two ways to log time — a live timer and a manual entry. Use whichever fits the moment; both count exactly the same on the suite side.

Running example — a Tuesday

§6.1–6.6 all follow the same day: you're a content writer with two active clients — Acme Roofing and Riverside Dental — and one task assigned to you on the Task board, "Write blog post — roof maintenance checklist" (Acme Roofing, estimated 3h).

6.1 — Starting a live timer (the normal case)

  1. Open the Everhour extension popup (or the web app).
  2. Pick the project — the client you're about to work on.
  3. Pick the task — the Everhour copy of your Task-board item (§04).
  4. Click Start. The extension icon changes, and the popup shows the live count.
  5. Do the work.
  6. Switching to something else? Click Stop — or just start a new timer; Everhour stops the old one automatically.

Why it matters

A live timer is the most accurate record there is — no guessing later "was that 45 minutes or an hour?" It's the best habit to build, especially for focused, single-task work.

Example

At 9:14 AM you open the extension, select Acme Roofing, select "Write blog post — roof maintenance checklist," and click Start. You write until 11:50 AM, then click Stop. Everhour has logged 2h 36m — no math required.

6.2 — Logging time after the fact

You'll forget to start the timer sometimes. That's fine — don't skip logging the time:

  1. Open My Timesheet (web app or the extension's day view).
  2. Find the day and the project/task row (or add one).
  3. Type in the hours — 1.5 for an hour and a half.
  4. Confirm/save.

Why it matters

A missing entry isn't neutral — it looks like zero time was spent, which understates that client's real cost and makes your utilization look artificially low. Log it even roughly; a good estimate beats nothing.

Example

At 1:00 PM you took a 45-minute call with Riverside Dental but never started a timer for it. That afternoon you open My Timesheet, find today under the Riverside Dental project (no specific task — it was a call), and type 0.75 into the hours field (45 minutes = 0.75h). Saved — the call is accounted for even though you forgot in the moment.

6.3 — Switching tasks mid-day

Just start the new timer — Everhour handles the handoff. Glance at the extension icon before you start something new; a timer already running against yesterday's task usually means you forgot to stop it (§6.6).

6.4 — Logging non-client / internal time

Meetings, training, internal admin — it still counts toward your utilization even though it doesn't belong to a client, so it shouldn't be logged under a client's project. Ask your PM which project or category the team uses for this. Don't leave it untracked, and don't log it under a client by mistake — that quietly inflates that client's real cost.

Example

Monday's 30-minute team standup isn't billable to any client. You log 0.5 under the team's Internal / Admin project — not under Acme Roofing just because that's the client you happened to be thinking about that morning, and not skipped just because "it's only a standup."

6.5 — Fixing a mistake

Wrong project, wrong day, wrong duration — it happens. In My Timesheet, click into the entry and edit or delete it directly. No approval step, no need to ask permission. Fix it as soon as you notice.

Example

On Wednesday you notice a 2-hour entry landed under Riverside Dental, but it was actually more blog-post work for Acme Roofing. You open that entry and change the project from Riverside Dental to Acme Roofing — no approval needed, just fix it.

6.6 — End of your work session

Before you close your laptop: check the extension icon. A timer left running overnight logs hours you didn't actually work — the single most common Everhour mistake. A 60-second scan of My Timesheet against what you remember doing is worth it.

Example

At 5:30 PM, about to close your laptop, you notice the extension icon shows a timer still running — from a Riverside Dental call that actually ended at 2:15 PM. You click Stop, then edit that entry's duration down from "3h 15m and counting" to the real ~20 minutes the call took.

6.7 — Billable vs. non-billable

Some entries carry a billable toggle. If your role involves marking this, your PM will give you the convention — default to the team standard unless told otherwise. The suite captures this flag today but doesn't yet split it into separate reporting, so getting it exactly right isn't mission-critical yet — but the habit will already be right the day it is.

Where your time lands — and why it's worth doing right

You never trigger anything — once a day, the suite quietly pulls the team's Everhour entries and turns them into things people actually look at:

Shows upLooks likeWhy it exists
On the task itself Est. 3h · Actual 4.5h Lets a PM see which tasks run over at a glance, and makes next month's estimates more honest.
Client workspace → Time card A rolling total of hours logged Gives account managers a real read on how much time a client actually takes.
Team / Workload page Logged hours vs. weekly capacity, as % How PACE knows who has room and who's stretched, so new work goes to someone with capacity.
Recipe Engine → Actual margin A real profitability number Turns "we think this client is profitable" into "we know."

None of this is about watching any one person — it's the difference between planning capacity and budgets off guesses versus off what actually happened. Accurate logging is what makes every one of those numbers trustworthy.

Example

By Friday, the task board shows "Write blog post — roof maintenance checklist" — Estimated 3h · Actual 3.1h — close enough that a similar post next time gets estimated the same way instead of padded "just in case." Acme Roofing's Time card for the month reads 18.25h logged. Your Workload page shows 86% utilization for the week — comfortably full, so PACE knows it's fine to hand you the next task instead of routing it elsewhere.

09

Quick reference

Every work session

  1. Open the extension → pick the client's project → pick the mirrored task → Start.
  2. Switching work? Just start the next timer.
  3. Forgot to start it? Log it manually the same day.
  4. End of day: check the icon — no timer should still be running.

Once, on day one

  • Confirm your account and login.
  • Confirm you're linked on the Team page.
  • Install + pin the extension, sign in.

Do

  • Track the mirrored task, not a new one.
  • Log honestly, even roughly.
  • Fix mistakes as soon as you spot them.

Never

  • Create your own tasks in Everhour.
  • Log client work to the wrong project.
  • Manage your to-do list from inside Everhour.
10

FAQ

Do I need to track every minute of my day?

Track your actual working time on client and internal work. You don't need to account for a bathroom break down to the second — the goal is an honest, close-enough picture, not a stopwatch on your whole life.

What if I genuinely don't remember how long something took?

Log your best honest estimate rather than nothing. Nothing reads as zero, which is worse than an approximate number.

My task isn't in Everhour yet — what do I do?

Give it a little time — the copy is created automatically but you may be looking before it syncs. Still missing? It may be a subtask (never mirrored) or an older task that predates the sync — ask your PM.

Can I see my own utilization number?

Ask your PM — the Team/Workload view is generally a PM/admin surface, but there's no reason you can't ask how you're tracking.

Does marking something "done" in Everhour close it out on the Task board?

No. They're not connected in that direction. Complete the task on the Task board itself.

Is this used to check up on me?

It's used to plan — client cost, team capacity, and estimate accuracy. Nobody is watching a live feed of your timer. Log honestly and it does its job quietly in the background.

Questions this guide doesn't answer? Ask your PM or admin — Everhour setup (accounts, project mapping, the suite link) is admin-side and usually a two-minute fix.
