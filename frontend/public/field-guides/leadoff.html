01

Before you start

LeadOff is the agency's pre-client market-selection tool — which city/category to pursue next, graded across tens of thousands of scanned markets. It has no per-client view of its own; the only bridge into the rest of the suite is "Create client from this market."

Anyone logged in can browse; spending money or creating a client needs staff/admin. Every paid action checks a shared $5.00/user/day budget first.

02

Browse the graded board

Sidebar → LeadOff. Sort by Opportunity (default), Grade, ROI, Profit, Expected $/mo, Expected leads, or Demand. Smart search resolves free text like "roofers in Cleveland" to a category + location in one call. HOT?/ COLD? badges flag demand 2× or 0.5× the city norm — worth a manual double-check. Beatability (Soft/Moderate/Tough) is a reading aid that never feeds the grade itself.

03

Read a Market Brief

Click a row: Economics (opportunity, expected $/mo, win likelihood, profit after costs, payback), Field forensics (Beatability, reviews to beat #3, the top-5 competitor list), Scouting report (links, review velocity, momentum — populated only after a Scout), and Proximity (octant coverage +, once GBP pins exist, the Placement Advisor's "best areas to plant a GBP").

04

Paid actions

Tryout~$0.20 typical, any US city ≥10k population not on the board
Scout~$0.10–1, fills in RD/review velocity/demand trend for a brief
Map refresh~$0.004, just re-pulls live competitor GBP pins

the badge is typical, not the reserve

Every estimate is shown before you commit, and the system reserves a worst-case amount against your daily budget — usually higher than the typical-cost badge.

05

Create a client

At the bottom of a Market Brief, "Create client from this market" — name required, website optional. Seeds the top-5 competitors into Competitive Intel and records the effort targets (reviews to beat #3, link budget) as a campaign goal, then opens the new client workspace.

06

Neighborhoods

A separate precomputed board of 955 nameable sub-area combos — pick these on demand and economics; supply is approximately the parent metro's.
