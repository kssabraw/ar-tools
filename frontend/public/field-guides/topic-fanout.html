01

Before you start

Topic Fanout is a separate app inside the dashboard (/fanout) — its own tab, same login and client context. What you see is decided automatically by your role, not a choice you make: owner (staff/ admin) gets the full power-user floor described below; va (team_member) gets a deliberately narrower wizard — no Articles tab, no free-form re-gating, a capped deep-mine, and a cost-approval step before anything spends real money.

Local SEO page scheduling needs a client with a Google Business Profile. Blog-post scheduling needs a site base URL — a client link is optional there, only used for auto-publish/WordPress. Nothing generates until you schedule it.

02

Start a new session

From the client workspace, click Create Mass Posts. Pick a content type — Blog content, or Local SEO content (requires a GBP-linked client).

Blog vs. Local SEO — which one. Blog is the default for informational/topical coverage — how-tos, comparisons, buyer-education pieces — and needs only a site base URL. Local SEO is for service × location pages ("emergency plumber Tampa") aimed at ranking in a specific place, so it only makes sense for a client whose business actually serves defined locations — that's why it requires the GBP link. Pick the wrong one and the batch is wasted spend: a national ecommerce client gets nothing from location pages, and a single-location trade business rarely needs 200 blog posts before it needs its service areas covered.

Fill in the seed form: Seed keyword (or Service), Location (Local SEO only), Search market, Silos (3–10, slider), Coverage mode (Standard top-5 / Comprehensive top-10), and a "Fetch volume/CPC/KD" checkbox.

Silos — how many. Fewer (3–4) keeps a run focused and cheaper — the right call for a narrow niche, or a first test run with a new client before you trust the pipeline's read on them. More (8–10) casts a wider net and suits a broad seed ("plumbing services") or a client you already trust to spend on volume. A second session with a different seed is cheap; an oversized first one isn't easily un-spent.

Coverage mode. Standard (top-5 competitors) is enough for most niches and keeps the pipeline faster and cheaper. Comprehensive (top-10) earns its extra cost in a genuinely competitive market, where the 6th–10th-ranked competitor's keyword coverage still differs meaningfully from the top 5 — in a sparse, low-competition niche it rarely turns up anything Standard didn't already find.

Fetch volume/CPC/KD. Turn it on when the plan needs to be prioritized by real demand and difficulty, not just topical relevance — worth it for a client-facing report, or before deciding which silos are worth deep-mining. Skip it for a quick internal test run where you just want to see what silo discovery finds.

Click Discover silos.

03

The run/deep-mine gate

Review the proposed silos — edit or remove any that don't fit — then Continue.

owner Two independent checkbox columns per silo: Run (which silos get expanded/clustered/planned at all) and Deep-mine (which of those additionally get competitor research; the seed silo is always mined).

va No Run-scope control at all — every silo always runs. You only pick which additional silos get deep-mined, capped at 2.

deep-mine costs more

It runs real competitor research per silo. Only mark the silos you actually want that depth on — the seed silo is deep-mined regardless of clearance.

Which silos to leave running. Un-check a silo you can already tell won't fit before spending anything on it — off-brand, too tangential, or duplicate of work already done elsewhere. When in doubt, leave it running: the cost that matters is in deep-mining and generation, not the keyword pipeline itself.

Which silos to deep-mine. Deep-mine the ones you're confident you'll actually schedule articles for — competitor research sharpens exactly those. Skip it on a silo you're including mostly for completeness or aren't sure you'll use; you can always come back and deep-mine it later if it turns out to matter.

Click Run keyword pipeline.

04

Watch the pipeline work

Expansion (~6–10 min) — keyword ideas/suggestions/PAA per silo, competitor keyword mining, relevance scoring, clustering. Finishes at "Keyword pipeline complete" with active/filtered counts; expand any silo to see its keywords. Click Plan articles for phase two (~1–4 min) — SERPs for candidate primary keywords, planning the actual articles per silo (merge/split/promote/drop), deduping across silos. Lands on "Article plan ready."

05

Review & tighten the plan

Tabs: Table (sortable/filterable keyword list) and Cluster (articles grouped by topic).

owner Fully editable — merge, split, edit, delete — plus a separate Split tab for architecture-level restructuring.

va Rename articles and move keywords between them; merge/split/delete are owner-only.

A gap-triage row can still appear but is largely historical — gaps auto-accept into keyword-named placeholder articles now.

owner Not happy with the pool? Open "Tighten the keyword pool" and click Re-gate — re-runs the relevance gate and clustering on keywords already collected, at no new spend, via tuning knobs (relevance threshold, cluster granularity, keywords per silo, edge threshold, silo margin). This clears the current article plan — Plan articles again afterward.

When to re-gate, and which knob. Too few keywords survived → loosen the relevance threshold, or widen the cluster granularity. Keywords feel off-topic or drifted → tighten the threshold instead. One or two silos ended up thin while others are crowded → raise their keywords-per-silo cap, or nudge the silo margin so a borderline keyword can populate more than one silo. It's free to experiment — only the initial keyword pipeline spent money; re-gating works the pool you already paid for.

06

Schedule content

Click Schedule (whole plan or a selected subset). Content type — Blog post, Local SEO page, or Service page. Client-linked sessions can turn on auto-publish to Drive and (if configured) WordPress. When: all at once, drip N/day or N/week or N/month, or a specific date. Maximum defaults to 1000 (blank = no limit) — pillar pages get priority under a cap, but only if the site architecture has already been generated; otherwise it takes the first N in plan order, with a banner saying so.

Content type at schedule. Blog post is the default for informational silos. Local SEO page is for a silo built around a service × location target. Service page is the narrower option — one offering's own page, not tied to a location — pick it when the silo is really about a single service the client wants a dedicated page for, not a spread of location or topic variants.

When — the cadence. All at once suits a small batch, a launch deadline, or a client who wants everything live together. Drip (N/day, N/week, N/month) is the better default for a large batch — it spreads the review/QA workload, avoids a suspicious overnight spike in a client's publishing history, and lets you catch a bad pattern after the first few before the rest generate. A specific date suits a coordinated launch (a new service line, a seasonal push) planned ahead of time.

Maximum. Leave it blank only once you've reviewed the full plan and want every article scheduled. Set a cap — especially on a first run with a new client, or a plan you haven't fully read through — to limit spend and give yourself a smaller batch to sanity-check before committing to the rest.

Auto-publish. Turn it on once you trust the pipeline's output for this client — an established relationship, a voice/ICP that's already dialed in — and want articles to land live with no manual step. Leave it off, the safer default for a new client or a first batch, so you can read each article in Articles before it goes anywhere.

The preview shows count, time, and cost. va a batch over the approval threshold shows a cost step and submits via "Submit for approval" rather than scheduling directly.

07

Track generated articles

owner only — this tab doesn't exist for a VA session. The Articles tab lists everything generated, with word count, cost, and Score. Per article: Read, and — for client-linked sessions — Score and Reoptimize (same pattern as Blog Writer's own). Toolbar: Download all (.zip), Push all to GitHub, Save N to Drive, publish to WordPress, and Publish settings.

A run that hit an error mid-pipeline, with its keyword pool saved, shows "Re-gate to recover" instead of "start over" — also owner-only. A VA session that errors always shows "Start a new session," regardless of whether the pool was saved.

08

Exports

Keyword research report (PDF) — exec summary, silos, demand data, top opportunities, content plan, full appendix; runs in the background, saves to Drive if linked. Export data — flat keyword list, topic-grouped export, site architecture, or an internal-linking edge list (the last two need architecture generated first).

09

Troubleshooting

How is this different from Keyword Research?That tool finds and understands what to target with one-off drafts; this one does its own silo discovery + pipeline from a seed. No built-in handoff between them.
My run errored — did I lose everything?Check for a saved keyword pool — "Re-gate to recover" reuses it at no cost (owner only).
I scheduled a batch and nothing happenedCheck whether it needed owner approval — held, not run silently.
Can I edit the article plan?Depends on clearance — owner can restructure, VA can rename/move only.
