## A. Business-model → SEO-strategy translation
SEO means different things per business type. For each type you work with, talk through:
- **The real goal** (what the client actually needs — leads, calls, revenue, AOV, sign-ups, ad impressions).
- **The metric that matters** (and the vanity metric that lies for this type).
- **The trap** — the mistake people make optimizing this type.
- **Seasonality / sales cycle** — how demand and conversion timing behave.
- **What "good" looks like** — a realistic win.

## B. Content quality judgment — beyond the rubric
The suite *scores* content on a rubric. You know the difference between "checks the boxes" and "genuinely the best result." Talk through:
- What makes a page **genuinely better** than the competitors ranking above it?
- **Information gain** — what does it mean to add something not already on page one, and how do you spot when content lacks it?
- **First-hand experience / originality** signals — what real experience looks like on a page vs. rephrased consensus.
- The **"would a human pick this as the best answer"** test — how you actually apply it.
- The **tells of box-checking content** (technically optimized, genuinely mediocre).
- What the **Helpful Content** shift rewards that a rubric can miss.
- How you judge whether content is **trustworthy** (E-E-A-T in practice, not in theory).

## C. Measurement & attribution wisdom
- Which **metrics lie**, and when? (rankings, impressions, traffic, DA/DR, "visibility scores.")
- **How long** do different changes realistically take to show — content refresh, new page, links, technical fix, GBP change?
- **Signal vs. noise** — how do you tell a real change from normal fluctuation? What size/duration of move do you trust?
- **Correlation vs. causation** traps you've seen people fall into.
- Why you **can't cleanly A/B test SEO**, and how you get evidence anyway.
- How do you know a **recovery is real** vs. a dead-cat bounce?
- **Attribution** — connecting SEO work to business outcomes (calls/leads/revenue) honestly, including the lag.
- The metrics you actually **report on** to an owner vs. the ones you watch internally.
