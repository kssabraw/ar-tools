The everyday guide to protein — how much you actually need

A plain-language look at the science: how your body uses protein, the myths worth ignoring, and simple ways to hit your target.
Dr. Maya Ellison
Reviewed by our editors · 12 min read · July 2026
[ hero photo: plated balanced meal ]

Protein is having a moment. It's on the front of cereal boxes, in every shake, and at the center of countless online arguments. Beneath the noise, the actual science is refreshingly settled — and simpler than the marketing suggests.

What protein actually does

Every tissue in your body — muscle, skin, enzymes, hormones — is built from proteins, which are in turn built from amino acids. When you eat protein, your body breaks it down into those building blocks and reassembles them into whatever it needs at the time.

Most healthy adults do well on 1.2–1.6 g of protein per kilogram of body weight per day.

How much you really need

The official minimum is set to prevent deficiency, not to optimize health. For most active people, aiming a bit higher — and spreading intake across the day — is a reasonable, low-risk approach that the evidence broadly supports.

You don't need to track every gram. A palm-sized portion of protein at each meal gets most people most of the way there without a spreadsheet.
